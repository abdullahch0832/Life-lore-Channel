# 10 — Script Writing, Explained Simply (for Phase‑1 videos)

This is the beginner‑friendly, complete guide to writing a Phase‑1 (6–15 min)
"curiosity" script — the kind that made "The Ocean is Way Deeper Than You Think" hit 50M.

## What a "script" actually is
A script is just **every word the narrator will say**, written down in order, with notes
in brackets telling the editor **what to show on screen** at each line. That's it. You
write the talking; the editor matches pictures to it.

Format looks like this:
```
VO: The ocean is really, really deep — deeper than most people realise.
[SHOW: zoom out from a beach into the deep blue ocean]

VO: If you shaved all the land off Earth and dumped it into the sea...
[SHOW: animation of mountains collapsing into the ocean]
```
`VO` = voiceover (what's said). `[SHOW: …]` = the visual cue.

## The ONE structure that powers these videos: **"The Ladder"**
Almost every big old RealLifeLore video is a **ladder**: you start small/normal, then
climb one step at a time, each step **more extreme than the last**, until a jaw‑dropping
climax — then end on a mystery. Here is the real "Ocean" video, broken into its 5 parts:

### Part 1 — The HOOK (first ~20 seconds)
Make one bold, simple claim + one mind‑bending fact. No slow intro.
> *"The ocean is really, really deep — deeper than most of us realise. If you shaved all
> the land off every continent and filled the deepest ocean with it, the whole Earth
> would still be under 2 miles of water."*

**Rule:** the viewer must feel "wait, WHAT?" in the first 15 seconds.

### Part 2 — The YARDSTICK (next ~15 seconds)
Give the audience a measuring stick they already understand, so the numbers mean
something.
> *"Let's start with scale. This dot is a human. This bigger dot is an elephant. This one
> is the largest ship ever built. Now let's start going down…"*

**Rule:** before you throw numbers, give something familiar to compare them to.

### Part 3 — The LADDER (the main body, ~70% of the video)
Now climb. Each rung = **a number + a familiar comparison + a bit more emotion.** In the
Ocean video, every step is a deeper depth:

| Depth | What it's compared to (the trick!) | Emotion |
|---|---|---|
| 40 m | max scuba diving depth | normal |
| 93 m | the Lusitania wreck (longer than the water is deep) | curious |
| 332 m | "111 m more and you'd reach the Empire State Building underwater" | awe |
| 500 m | deepest a Blue Whale dives | awe |
| 1,000 m | "the scary zone — no light reaches here ever again" | **fear** |
|  | pressure = "a polar bear standing on a quarter" | fear |
| 2,000 m | the Black Dragonfish & Giant Squid live here | fear |
| 3,800 m | the Titanic wreck | awe |
| 6,000 m | the "Hadal Zone", named after Hades the underworld; pressure = "an elephant on a postage stamp, or one person holding 50 jumbo jets" | dread |
| 8,848 m | Mt. Everest, but upside‑down and underwater | mind‑blown |
| 10,994 m | the bottom: Challenger Deep | climax |

See the pattern? **Never just say "1,000 metres."** Always: *1,000 metres — that's as
tall as X, and the pressure would do Y to you.* That one habit is 80% of the magic.

### Part 4 — The CLIMAX (~15 seconds)
Hit the biggest, most extreme point. Let it land.
> *"Finally, at 10,994 metres, we reach the very bottom of the known ocean — the
> Challenger Deep."*

### Part 5 — The OPEN‑ENDED MYSTERY ending (~20 seconds)
Don't wrap it in a neat bow. Leave the viewer with wonder (this drives comments + the
next click).
> *"Only about 5% of the ocean floor has been properly mapped. The other 95% is still a
> mystery — and there are almost certainly even deeper places we simply haven't found
> yet."*

## The 6 golden rules (memorise these)
1. **Hook in 15 seconds.** Bold claim + one shocking fact. Never a slow "hello guys".
2. **Every sentence = a number OR a comparison.** Facts, not opinions. Density = authority.
3. **Always translate numbers into things people know.** "830 m = the Burj Khalifa."
   "Pressure = an elephant on a postage stamp." This is the single most important habit.
4. **Escalate.** Each step must feel bigger/scarier/weirder than the one before. Order
   matters — small → extreme.
5. **One idea only.** The whole video answers ONE simple question. Don't wander.
6. **End on a mystery**, not a conclusion. Wonder → rewatch, comment, subscribe.

## How to WRITE one, step by step
1. **Pick one simple question** from `docs/09` (e.g. *"How deep can humans go underwater
   before it kills them?"*).
2. **Collect 10–20 "rungs"** — real facts/milestones, from small to extreme. Research
   each from Wikipedia / NASA / NOAA / World Bank etc. **Write the source next to each.**
3. **Order them** smallest → most extreme.
4. **Write the hook** (Part 1) — the boldest, simplest version of your question + 1 wow fact.
5. **Write the yardstick** (Part 2) — one familiar comparison to anchor the audience.
6. **Write each rung** (Part 3): `number + familiar comparison + one line of emotion`.
   Add a little "scary/weird" escalation as you go deeper/bigger.
7. **Write the climax** (Part 4) and the **mystery ending** (Part 5).
8. **Add `[SHOW: …]` cues** on every line for the editor.
9. **Read it out loud.** Cut anything boring, especially in the first 30 seconds. Target
   ~150 words per minute (so a 7‑minute video ≈ 1,000–1,100 words).
10. **Fact‑check every number** before it goes to edit. This protects your channel.
